Krates — Terms of Use

Last updated: 21 September 2026

These terms cover your use of the Krates iPhone app. They are written to be read, not to be survived. If something here is unclear, ask.

What Krates is

Krates reads medical records you have already connected on your own device, charts them over time, and points out results that may be worth raising with a clinician. It reads; it never writes anything back to a health system or to Apple Health.

Using it means you agree to these terms. If you do not, delete the app — that is the whole of the arrangement.

Krates is not medical advice

Krates is not a medical device, and nothing in it is a diagnosis, a treatment recommendation, or a substitute for a clinician. It makes observations about records you already hold: that a value has moved, that a test has not been repeated, that a pattern is worth a question. Deciding what any of that means is a doctor's job, not an app's.

Do not use Krates to decide whether to start, stop or change a medication, and do not use it to decide whether to seek care. If you think something is wrong, contact a clinician. In an emergency, call emergency services.

Your records, and their limits

Krates shows you data supplied by Apple Health and by the health systems you connect. It does not create that data and cannot correct it. Records can be incomplete, delayed, out of date, duplicated, or wrong at the source, and a health system may hold results Krates never receives. Findings computed from incomplete data can be incomplete in turn.

Reference ranges come from the performing laboratory where one is supplied. Where none is, the app substitutes a general-purpose range and says so on the card. A general range is a display aid, not a clinical judgement about you.

What you need

You need an iPhone and records you are entitled to read — your own, or those of someone you are legally responsible for, such as your child or a person whose care you manage. Do not use Krates to read anyone else's record.

You are responsible for the device the records sit on. Krates stores them locally, protected by your device passcode and Apple's file encryption; if you share your unlocked phone, you share the record.

The optional wording feature

The sentences on a finding card, and answers to questions you type, are written by a language model, and this sends the measurements behind that one finding off your device. A screen on first run shows exactly what would be sent, before anything is sent, and it can be switched off at any time. The app is fully usable with it off.

Language models make mistakes, including confident ones. Every number on a card is computed on your device from your own records, but the sentences around those numbers are generated and should be read as explanation, not as fact checked by a person.
